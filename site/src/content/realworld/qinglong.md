claim: |
  The Azure Dragon (青龙/苍龙) is a real astronomical constellation — the eastern quadrant of the Chinese sky, composed of seven specific lunar mansions that trace a dragon's shape through the spring and summer stars. Chinese astronomy tracked the Azure Dragon's rising and setting to mark the seasons for millennia before the myth became a divine creature.
evidence: |
  Chinese traditional astronomy divides the sky into four quadrants, each governed by one of the Four Symbols: Azure Dragon (East), White Tiger (West), Vermilion Bird (South), Black Tortoise (North). The Azure Dragon quadrant (东方青龙) encompasses seven lunar mansions — 角 (Horn), 亢 (Neck), 氐 (Root), 房 (Room), 心 (Heart), 尾 (Tail), 箕 (Winnowing Basket) — which correspond to identifiable stars in the modern constellations of Virgo, Libra, Scorpius, and Sagittarius. The Mawangdui astronomical silk manuscripts (c. 168 BCE) include representations of the four quadrant creatures; the *Shiji*'s "Treatise on the Celestial Offices" (天官书, c. 90 BCE) describes the Azure Dragon's stars in systematic positional terms. The rising of the Azure Dragon's "Heart" mansion (心宿, corresponding to Antares in Scorpius) in the east marked the beginning of summer in ancient Chinese calendrical astronomy.
scholars_say: |
  The four-quadrant system is confirmed as genuine historical astronomy by both Chinese and Western scholarship. Joseph Needham's *Science and Civilisation in China* (Vol. 3) documents the 28 lunar mansions as a positional coordinate system independent of but parallel to the Babylonian zodiac, with the Azure Dragon quadrant (mansions 1–7) corresponding precisely to identifiable bright stars. The seasonal functions are documented in the *Yueling* (月令) calendar texts: the Azure Dragon rises in the east in spring, confirming its eastern directional assignment. What is mythological is the dragon body, the divine personality, and the five-phase associations; what is astronomical is the seven-mansion star group that has been tracked by Chinese observers for at least 2,500 years.

The Azure Dragon is the clearest example in Chinese mythology of a creature that is simultaneously a divine being and a positional astronomy framework. The seven lunar mansions of the eastern quadrant are real stars with documented positions, used to mark the seasons and orient navigation for at least 2,500 years. The creature that those stars are said to trace is mythology. The stars themselves are not.

The distinction matters because it runs in both directions: dismissing the Azure Dragon as "just mythology" misses 2,500 years of observational astronomy; accepting it as a literal dragon in the sky misses the systematic positional science that the quadrant system was built to provide. Chinese astronomical tradition held both simultaneously, and both remain available to anyone who looks east in spring.
