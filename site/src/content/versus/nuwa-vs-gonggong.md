## The Case for Nüwa

Nüwa's résumé ends with the sky intact. That is the operational bottom line of the *Huainanzi*'s account: she smelted five-colored stones and patched the breach in heaven, propped the four cardinal poles on a tortoise's legs, killed the black dragon that was ravaging the land, and dammed the floods with reed ash. The *Huainanzi*'s text does not mention a culprit — the sky simply collapsed, the way overstressed structures collapse, and Nüwa repaired it. She is the crisis manager who left the world in better condition than she found it.

Her power is structural: she is one of the few figures in Chinese mythology who both *made* the world (the human-creation tradition preserved in the *Fengsu Tongyi*) and *maintained* it. The sky-repair is not a battle — it is engineering, and Nüwa is its author. Where Gonggong creates a catastrophe from a personal defeat, Nüwa creates a solution from nothing but craft and will. The fire she used to smelt the stones; the tortoise she killed for its legs; the dragon she destroyed as a side-task — these are instruments of a thoroughgoing restoration that Gonggong's head-butt never deserved.

## The Case for Gonggong

Gonggong's act needs no defense because it needs no justification — it happened, and nothing undid it. The *Huainanzi* describes his collision with Mount Buzhou in passing, almost as geographic etymology:

> 昔者共工与颛顼争为帝，怒而触不周之山，天柱折，地维绝。天倾西北，故日月星辰移焉；地不满东南，故水潦尘埃归焉。
>
> "In former times Gonggong and Zhuanxu contended for the throne. In rage, Gonggong charged into Mount Buzhou. The pillar of heaven broke; the cords of the earth snapped. Heaven tilted to the northwest — which is why the sun, moon, and stars move in that direction. Earth became hollow in the southeast — which is why water and silt flow there."

Every river in China still runs southeast. The sun still rises and sets in the direction Gonggong's tantrum established. His loss is the permanent structure of the cosmos. He is the most consequential failure in Chinese mythology: he lost the throne and rewrote the sky in the process. The fact that Nüwa repaired the sky does not undo that the rivers are still running where his crash left them.

## The Verdict

**Nüwa — but only on the question of restoration.** The *Huainanzi* does not actually stage a confrontation between them; their stories are connected by consequence, not by meeting. Gonggong breaks the sky in one passage; Nüwa repairs it in another. Later tradition assembled them into a sequence, which has become the standard account. But the honest reading of the primary texts shows two separate figures operating in the same cosmological space without witnessing each other.

On pure capability: Nüwa's repair is more impressive than Gonggong's damage. It takes more craft to patch heaven with five-colored stones than to crack it with a skull. But Gonggong's legacy is the more durable: his rivers are still draining southeast. His tilted sky still rises in the east. Nüwa's patch held — but the underlying geometry that Gonggong imposed when he failed is the one we still navigate.

**Winner: Nüwa on the sky. Gonggong on the world's drainage.**
