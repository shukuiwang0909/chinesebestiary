## The Case for Kuafu

Start with the feat no other being in the canon can match: **he ran down the sun**. Not symbolically — the *Classic of the Great Wilderness: North* says he caught up with the sun's shadow at the Valley of Yu. In between strides he drained the Yellow River and the Wei in single draughts, and when those weren't enough he still had strength left to reach for the Great Marsh. He wears the regalia of a divine giant — four live snakes — and his cast-off staff became a forest. This is a being of literal cosmic endurance, with a giant's strength to match his giant's thirst.

Against anything that can be caught, cornered, or outlasted, Kuafu is the most terrifying opponent in the bestiary. The problem is only who he drew.

## The Case for Zhulong

Zhulong — Torch Dragon, Candle Dragon — does not chase the sun, because Zhulong is the reason the chase means anything. **When he opens his eyes, it is day. When he closes them, night falls.** His breath is the wind; his in-breath makes winter, his out-breath makes summer. He neither eats, nor drinks, nor sleeps — which means he has no thirst to exploit, no fatigue to outlast, no blind moment to ambush. Kuafu's one historical weakness killed him precisely because it was the one resource Zhulong does not consume.

Kuafu caught the sun's *shadow* once. Zhulong is what casts it.

## The Verdict

**Zhulong, without breaking a sweat — which is itself the point.** Kuafu's legend is a story about reaching; Zhulong is the destination. Even granting Kuafu every giant feat in the text, he cannot win a contest of attrition against a being that does not eat, drink, or sleep, and he cannot meaningfully "catch" a creature whose blink is the day-night cycle. The best Kuafu can hope for is what he got from the sun itself: the honor of almost. **Winner: Zhulong, by existing.**
