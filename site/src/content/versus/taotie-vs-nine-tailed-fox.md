## The Case for Taotie

Strip away two thousand years of bronzeware symbolism and the Taotie is one thing: an eating machine with no off switch. The *Shanhaijing* gives it a human face, tiger fangs, human claws, and — crucially — **eyes beneath its forelegs**. Nothing ambushes it; the fox's entire toolkit of illusion, misdirection, and nine-tail feints is watched from four directions at once.

Then there is the appetite. The Taotie swallowed people whole and was *still* not sated. It does not negotiate, it does not sleep on the decision, and it is immune to the only weapon the fox truly has — because you cannot charm a creature that does not want anything from you except your calories. Daji toppled a dynasty over years. The Taotie would have toppled Daji by Thursday.

## The Case for the Nine-Tailed Fox

The fox has won exactly one kind of fight, over and over, for three millennia: the fight that happens *before* the fight. Her cry lures prey like the Taotie's; her difference is what happens next. Kings, monks, and whole dynasties have guarded her interests voluntarily. In a city like Zhaoge she arrives with an army of the enchanted, plus — per later tradition — shapeshifting, foxfire, and a thousand years of accumulated cunning.

And do not discount the poison immunity: eating fox flesh shields against gu hexes, which hints at a constitution built for surviving exactly the kind of treachery-heavy battlefield she engineers. If she chooses the ground, she does not need to out-bite the Taotie. She needs someone else to do it for her.

## The Verdict

**Taotie takes it — if the fox is ever foolish enough to be in the same room.** Every path to victory for the fox requires time, proxies, and distance; the Taotie's armpit eyes close the distance problem, and its bottomless gullet answers the proxy problem by eating the proxies. The fox's real power was never combat — it was surviving every era that tried to demonize her, which is its own kind of victory, but not this one. **Winner: Taotie, by first contact.**
