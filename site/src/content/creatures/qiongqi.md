## Two Faces, One Monster

The *Classic of Mountains and Seas* never reconciles its two Qiongqis. The Regions Within the Seas (海内北经) describes a winged tiger that devours people head-first — one variant text says beginning at the feet — leaving its victims with hair hanging loose. The Mountains of the West (西次四经) sends travelers 260 li further west to Mount Gui, where a beast shaped like an ox, bristling with hedgehog quills and howling like a hound, answers to the same name. Two bodies, one monster: the compilers apparently stitched together regional traditions without choosing between them.

## From Bad Son to Bad Omen

The *Zuo Zhuan* (左传), a chronicle compiled in the 3rd century BCE, gives Qiongqi a human origin: a worthless son of the Shaohao clan who broke faith, hated loyalty, and trafficked in slander until the people of the world named him for the beast. By Han times this had fused with the legend of the "four evil clans" that the sage-king Shun exiled to the four frontiers. The *Huainanzi* (淮南子, c. 139 BCE) supplies a fitting lineage: Qiongqi is the offspring of the Guangmo (广莫), the northwest wind — one reason later bestiaries file it under the element of wind.

## The Beast That Punishes the Righteous

The *Shen Yi Jing* (神异经), a Han compendium attributed to the court wit Dongfang Shuo, completes the inversion. This Qiongqi understands human speech: when it hears a quarrel, it devours whichever party is in the right; hearing of loyalty and good faith, it bites off the speaker's nose; hearing of cruelty, it slaughters an animal and carries the meat to the wicked as a gift. Justice turned perfectly upside down — and a warning that in a disordered age, virtue itself becomes prey.

## One of the Four Perils

With Taotie (饕餮), Hundun (混沌), and Taowu (梼杌), Qiongqi anchors the roster of the Four Perils — the anti-pantheon a ruler must exile to keep the realm in order. If the Four Divine creatures (四灵) embody harmony, the Four Perils embody its opposites: chaos, gluttony, obstinate ignorance, and perverted justice.
