## Mount Zhaoyao's Bestiary

The Chiru does not appear alone. Mount Zhaoyao (招摇之山), in the third part of the southern mountain chain, is one of the Shanhaijing's richest single entries — a mountain that overlooks the West Sea and contains several notable creatures and plants in a single paragraph. Alongside the Chiru, the entry describes:

- **Zhuyu grass** (祝余): leek-shaped, blue flowers; eating it prevents hunger
- **Migu tree** (迷谷): paper-mulberry form, black grain, flowers that illuminate all four directions; wearing it prevents getting lost
- **Xingxing ape** (猩猩): the white-eared ape with human speech and a wine-weakness; eating it makes one run fast
- **Yupei** (育沛): a water substance; wearing it prevents growths and tumors
- **Chiru** (赤鱬): the human-faced carp; eating it prevents scabies

Five different types of benefit in a single mountain: anti-hunger, anti-disorientation, speed, tumor prevention, skin disease cure. Zhaoyao Mountain reads like a physician's supply depot. The Shanhaijing's southern chain has a notably medical character — perhaps reflecting the real botanical and zoological knowledge of southern Chinese and Southeast Asian traders and explorers who contributed to the text's composition.

## Carp With a Face

The carp (鲤 *lǐ*) occupies a specific cultural position in Chinese tradition: it is the most auspicious freshwater fish, the one that becomes a dragon if it leaps the Dragon Gate, the one given as a gift and raised in garden ponds as a sign of prosperity. The Shanhaijing's Chiru takes this familiar, domesticated fish form and adds a human face — small, alert, looking directly at you from a fish's body.

The human face is a consistent Shanhaijing marker for creatures that belong to a hybrid zone between human and animal. Most fish in the text are described purely as fish. A fish with a human face has crossed a threshold: it can look back at you, it can recognize you, its call is described in terms of a bird's recognizable sound rather than a generic noise. The Chiru is a fish that exists in relationship — it sees you, it can be heard, and if you eat it, it cures you.

See [Xingxing](/creatures/xingxing/) for the ape encountered on the same mountain; see [Yingyu](/creatures/yingyu/) for the other remarkable fish of the southern chain.
