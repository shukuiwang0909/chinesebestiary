## What 尸 Means

The character 尸 (*shī*) carries a heavy modern connotation — in contemporary Chinese it means "corpse." But in the archaic ritual world that produced the *Shanhaijing*, it meant something quite different: the sacred stand-in (神主) for a deity during sacrifice.

The Zhou ritual system, documented in the *Book of Rites* and the *Ceremonies*, required that major ancestral sacrifices include a living 尸 — a descendant of the deceased, or a designated representative, who would sit in the ritual space and receive the offerings on behalf of the ancestor. The 尸 was not pretending to be the ancestor; in the ritual logic of the time, the ancestor was actually present *through* the 尸. The offerings reached the ancestor *through* this living, physical intermediary.

When the *Shanhaijing* says 奢比之尸 — "the 尸 of Shebi" — it is saying: this is the point in the eastern world where the deity Shebi is manifest, where Shebi is physically present and available to be addressed. The beast body is Shebi's divine body. The human face is Shebi's face turned toward worshippers. The large ears are Shebi's capacity to hear.

## The Snake-Earring Family

Green snake earrings (珥两青蛇) appear on both Shebizhi in the east and Yuqiang in the north. Yellow snake earrings appear on Yuguo in the adjacent eastern cosmography. This is not coincidence — it is a consistent iconographic vocabulary. Wearing snakes is the *Shanhaijing*'s visual grammar for a deity of commanding divine authority. The snakes are alive and they stay in place because they obey. They are not decoration; they are the deity's relationship with the wild world made visible as ornament.

Shebizhi's large ears amplify this: a deity that can hear prayers from great distances, whose awareness extends into the supernatural, whose presence in the world is felt by those who know to look for it.

## Eastern Anchor

The *Classic of Regions Beyond the Seas: East* places Shebizhi north of the Junzi (Gentleman) state — a location at the edge of the eastern world. As Yuqiang anchors the north, Shebizhi anchors the east. The two share the green-snake earring iconography, perhaps marking them as belonging to the same divine tier: the gods who stand at the cardinal directions and keep the world's edges in order.
