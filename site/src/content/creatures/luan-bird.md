## A Pheasant of Five Colors

The *Classic of the Mountains: West* gives the Luan Bird a single sentence on Mount Nüchuang (女床之山): shaped like a long-tailed pheasant, patterned in five colors — and *when it appears, all under heaven is at peace* (见则天下安宁). The formula is identical to the one the southern mountains use for the fenghuang. Two birds, two ranges, one promise: the cosmos keeps omen-birds on call, and their plumage is the certificate of a rightly ordered world.

The difference is in the detail. The fenghuang wears the five virtues as legible characters on its head, wings, back, breast, and belly — a bird you can read. The luan wears only the five colors, pattern without text. Tradition took the hint and ranked them: the fenghuang sovereign, the luan its herald and companion, the bird that announces rather than the bird that is announced.

## Singer in the Wilds of Wo

The *Classic of Regions Beyond the Seas: West* shows the luan at home. In the Wilds of Wo (沃之野), a paradise west of the known world, "luan birds sang of their own accord; phoenixes danced of their own accord" — and the people ate phoenix eggs and drank sweet dew, wanting for nothing. The luan's song is not a performance; it is the background music of a world functioning correctly. Where the bird sings unprompted, the age is good.

## From Omen to Ornament

Later centuries domesticated the omen into an ornament. Because ritual chariots carried ringing bells called luan (鸾/銮) — already ringing "in harmony" in the *Classic of Poetry* — the bird's name fused with the emperor's equipage: 銮驾, "the luan carriage," became a way to say *the emperor is passing*. Qu Yuan, in the *Li Sao*, summons the luan and the phoenix to fly ahead of his spirit-chariot as heralds. The omen-bird of the western mountains ended up, like many gods, working in protocol — announcing arrivals.

## Modern Echoes

Of all the creatures in this bestiary, the Luan Bird has taken the strangest flight. In AVIC's "[Nantianmen Project](/articles/nantianmen-project/)" — a science-fiction universe first shown publicly in 2017 — the Luan Bird reappears as a colossal airborne carrier, a 120,000-ton sky-fortress mother ship carrying scores of unmanned space fighters. What was once an omen of peace ("when the Luan Bird appears, the world is at ease") became, in twenty-first-century aerospace fiction, a vessel of deterrence. Whether read as worldbuilding, wishful technology roadmap, or state-sanctioned mythmaking, the omen-bird of the Classic of Mountains and Seas has acquired engines.

The Luan Bird is one officeholder in a much larger celestial bureaucracy — see [Why China Names Its Spacecraft After Mythology](/articles/why-china-names-spacecraft-after-mythology/).
