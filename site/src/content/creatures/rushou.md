## One Line in the West

The *Classic of Regions Beyond the Seas: West* gives Rushou his seven characters (西方蓐收，左耳有蛇，乘两龙): he presides over the west, a serpent threads his left ear, and two dragons carry him. With Goumang of the east, Zhurong of the south, and Yuqiang of the north, he completes the Classic's matched set of directional gods — same chassis, different trim. His badges are the strangest of the four: not wings or a beast body, but a snake through the ear and the axe no text mentions yet.

## Metal Rectifier

The office came, as with Goumang and Zhurong, from the *Zuo Zhuan*: among the five rectifiers, the Metal Rectifier is called Rushou — metal being the phase of the west, of autumn, of white, of the blade. The same passage names his clan: the four younger sons of Shaohao were Chong, Gai, Xiu, and Xi, and to them fell metal, wood, and water — Chong serving as Goumang of wood, Gai as Rushou of metal, Xiu and Xi together as Xuanming of water. The *Monthly Ordinances* turned the office into ritual: in autumn's months the ruler greets the west in white, rewards the harvesters, sharpens the laws — and carries out the executions that summer's growth-season had forbidden.

## The Dream at the Western Wall

Rushou's single eyewitness description comes from a nightmare. A duke of Guo dreamed he was in his ancestral temple when a god appeared: a human face, hair white as autumn frost, the claws of a tiger, a great battle-axe in hand, standing in the western corner. The duke fled; the god called out — do not run, for Heaven's decree commands that Jin shall fall upon your gates. Waking in terror, the duke summoned his diviner, who heard the description and named the apparition at once: that was Rushou, he said, the god of celestial punishment (天之刑神). The minister Shi Yin, told the omen, answered with the verdict history proved: a state that listens to spirits instead of its people is already finished. Guo fell to Jin, and Rushou's white-haired silhouette entered the Chinese imagination as the face of a doom already signed.

## White Tiger of the West

Han cosmology completed the portrait the dream had begun. The west is the quarter of metal, the color white, the season of cutting down; and its beast, from Han times onward, is the White Tiger (白虎) — lord of war and executioners, the soldier's star. Rushou's tiger claws, his axe, and his office fit the same character: of the five rectifiers he is the only one whose season ends things. Autumn is not cruel in the five-phase scheme; it is just — the harvest gathered, the accounts closed, the condemned executed. Goumang presides over what begins; Rushou, over what is finished.
