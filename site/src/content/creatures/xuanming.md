## Not in the Classic — and That Is the Point

A careful note first, because this site promises them: Xuanming does not appear in the *Classic of Mountains and Seas*. Search its mountains and wastes and you will find the north's water god there — Yuqiang, human-faced and bird-bodied, serpents through his ears, standing on two serpents at the frozen edge of the map. Xuanming belongs to a different, slightly later filing system: the state religion of the rectifiers, where heaven's bureaucracy was sorted into five offices held by five gods. He is what the Classic's wilderness looks like after it has been given a desk in the capital.

## The Water Rectifier

The *Zuo Zhuan* supplies the office in the same breath as the others: the Wood Rectifier is Goumang, the Fire Rectifier Zhurong, the Metal Rectifier Rushou, the Water Rectifier Xuanming, the Earth Rectifier Houtu. Xuanming's entry alone names two holders at once — Xiu and Xi, brothers of the Shaohao clan, who served as Xuanming together. Water, it seems, needed double coverage: it is the phase that both gives life and takes it, and the only one that can drown the world. Where Gonggong is water as catastrophe, Xuanming is water as administration.

## Winter's God

The *Monthly Ordinances* of the Book of Rites give him his calendar: in early winter the ruler retires to his inner chambers, repairs walls and dikes, buries the harvest, closes the passes — and honors Xuanming, whose sovereign for the season is Zhuanxu, the high god of the north. The *Huainanzi* completes the court with its beast: the Dark Warrior (玄武), a tortoise entwined with a snake, black-armored sentinel of the northern palace. Later ages blurred god and beast into one another until Xuanming, Xuanwu, and the black tortoise-snake traded attributes freely — a fusion this entry keeps honestly apart.

## The Dark Obscure

His name became his afterlife. 玄冥 — the Dark Obscure — turned into a common noun for everything black-still: the heart of winter, the bottom of the northern sea, and, in centuries of poets, the dim country of the dead. A god dissolves upward into his season the way winter does into the year; Xuanming today survives less as a figure than as a temperature — the point where water, darkness, and death stop being three things.
