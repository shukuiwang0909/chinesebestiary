## A Dragon That Is Also a Calendar

The Azure Dragon's body is mapped across thirty-two asterisms in the eastern sky. When its horn rises above the eastern horizon at dusk in early spring, farmers know it is time to plant. When its heart (the star Antares) transits the meridian, it is midsummer. When its tail sets in the west as the sun rises, autumn has come. The Four Symbols are not myths imposed on stars — they are a reading system, a way of using the sky's motion to track the year.

## Not a Monster

Chinese dragons are not the treasure-hoarding, fire-breathing enemies of the European tradition. They are transformative, benevolent, and associated with water — rain, rivers, the sea. The Azure Dragon in particular governs beneficial moisture: the spring rains that make crops possible, the rivers that carry commerce, the wells that sustain cities. Its anger is rare; its blessing is the default. This is why the dragon became the imperial symbol: not fearsome power over people but the power that makes life possible.

## East and Left

In Chinese orientation, east is always associated with the left — when you stand facing south (the direction of authority), the east is to your left. The Azure Dragon, guardian of the east, is therefore also the Dragon of the Left. In feng shui the left side of any site should be slightly higher than the right — the Dragon side outranking the Tiger side, spring outranking autumn, beginning outranking ending.

## Japan's Seiryu

The Azure Dragon crossed to Korea and Japan early, arriving during the period of Tang cultural influence. In Japan it became Seiryu (青龍), one of the four divine beasts guarding the ancient capital at Nara, and remains one of the most recognizable mythological creatures in Japanese popular culture — appearing in Final Fantasy, Street Fighter, and dozens of anime series. The creature traveled twelve hundred kilometers and fifteen hundred years and retained its essential nature: the eastern sky, ascending, in spring.
