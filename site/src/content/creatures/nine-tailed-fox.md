## The Original Warning

The earliest stratum of the *Classic of Mountains and Seas* does not flatter the nine-tailed fox. It is a predator of the eastern mountains, and its infant-like cry is a hunting lure. Eating its flesh, however, grants immunity to gu poison — an early sign that this creature straddles the line between danger and blessing.

## From Omen to Seductress

By the Han dynasty, the fox had become an auspicious sign. The *Bamboo Annals* records that a nine-tailed fox appeared to Yu the Great before he founded the Xia dynasty — a divine endorsement of his rule. Tang dynasty tales (*Guangyi Ji*) began the transformation into the beautiful fox-spirit, a lineage that runs through Pu Songling's *Strange Tales* and into the present day.

## One Fox, Three Mythologies

The nine-tailed fox is the shared ancestor of East Asia's fox traditions: China's *huli jing*, Japan's *kitsune*, and Korea's *kumiho*. Each culture drew a different moral from the same animal — wisdom in Japan, danger in Korea, and a shifting mirror of virtue and vice in China.
