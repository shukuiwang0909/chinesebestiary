## Five Attributes, One Benefit

The Lushu's entry in the *Classic of the Mountains: South, Part II* is twenty-two characters in the original. This brevity is characteristic of the Shanhaijing — most creatures receive only a line or two — but the compression is efficient. In those twenty-two characters, the Lushu is given five physical attributes and one practical benefit:

**Horse-shaped** — the base form: size, movement, the category of large powerful quadruped.  
**White head** — the unusual detail, the mark of distinctiveness; a horse-shaped creature with an entirely pale head stands out.  
**Tiger markings** — the coat pattern, bold stripes on the body; the Lushu borrows the tiger's visual authority.  
**Red tail** — the third color-marker, vivid and vital, blazing at the animal's rear.  
**Song-like cry** — not a neigh, not a roar: a sound described with the character 谣, which means a ballad or folk tune.

Then the benefit: 佩之宜子孙. Wear it, and your descendants will be auspicious. The benefit is specific: not health, not wealth, not protection — but the family line. Progeny. The continuation of yourself through children and grandchildren.

## The Logic of the Amulet

The Shanhaijing records two kinds of benefit from its creatures: things to eat and things to wear. Eating a creature's flesh or organs typically confers a physical or behavioral effect — run faster, resist illness, avoid getting lost. Wearing a creature confers a different kind of benefit — protective, social, or relational.

Guo Pu's commentary notes that 佩之 (wearing it) means making an ornament from part of the Lushu — its hide, hair, or bone — and carrying that ornament. This is the logic of sympathetic transfer: an animal of exceptional vitality, hybrid power, and musical harmony has life-force to spare, and that excess transfers to the person who carries a piece of it.

The child-blessing function is consistent with the Lushu's attributes. A horse's endurance, a tiger's strength, a red tail's vital fire, and a musical voice: these are the qualities the Chinese classical tradition associated with a prosperous family — robust, strong, harmonious, and full of life. The Lushu doesn't *do* anything in the encounter; it simply *is*, and being in relationship with it (carrying a fragment) is enough to benefit from what it is.

## The Sound

The detail that stands apart is the voice. The Shanhaijing's creatures rarely have musical cries — most sounds are described with functional animal terms (like a dog, like a crow, like a bell). 谣 is a human musical category: folk songs, ballads, improvisational poetry. A horse-shaped beast that sounds like it is singing is one of the text's gentler uncanny moments — not threatening, not strange in a frightening way, but wrong in a way that is beautiful.
