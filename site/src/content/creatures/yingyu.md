## Fish That Flies

The *Shanhaijing* records a short entry that contains more strangeness per character than almost anything else in the text:

> There is a fish shaped like a carp, with a fish body and bird wings, blue-green markings and a white head with a red beak. It habitually travels in the West Sea and swims in the East Sea, and flies at night. Its name is Yingyu; its cry is its own name. Seeing it means the world will suffer great drought.

Seven clauses. Seven pieces of information, each one adding a layer of wrongness to the picture. By the time you reach the omen — *seeing it means world drought* — the creature has already told you everything you need to understand why. A fish with wings. A carp with a beak. A water creature that flies at night between two seas at opposite ends of the world. The Yingyu is drought made biological: the category of "water creature" has broken down, and so has the water cycle.

## The Self-Named Call

"Its cry is its own name" (其鸣自訆) is one of the Shanhaijing's most resonant formulas. It appears for several creatures across the text, and it always carries the same implication: the creature is so completely what it is that its sound *is* its identity. To hear the Yingyu is to hear the word 嬴鱼 — the fish naming itself in the air.

This is a mythological statement about the relationship between name and nature. In the cosmological tradition that the Shanhaijing preserves, naming is an act of power: to name something correctly is to know it completely. The Yingyu cannot be named by someone else because it has already named itself. And what it says, calling its own name into the night sky, is an announcement: *I am here. Drought is coming.*

## The World-Scale Omen

Most Shanhaijing omens are local: seeing a certain creature means this region will have war, or this valley will have flood. The Yingyu's omen is not local. 天下大旱: "great drought under all heaven." The whole world.

This is consistent with the creature's range. The Yingyu does not live in one sea — it commutes between the East and West, traveling the full extent of the known world every night. Its territory is the entire globe's ocean system. When a creature that vast says drought is coming, it is not predicting a dry season. It is predicting the failure of the world's water.

The Yingyu, flying between the seas at night, crying its own name into the dark — it is the loneliest creature in the Shanhaijing, and the one with the worst news.
