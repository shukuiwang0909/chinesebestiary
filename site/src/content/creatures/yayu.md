## A Monster With a Backstory

Most monsters in the *Shanhaijing* arrive without history: they are there, they are dangerous, they are described. Yayu is different. Its entry in the *Classic of Regions Within the Seas: West* begins not with a threat but with a crime:

> Yayu has a snake body and human face; it is the creature that the god Erfu killed.

The text then pivots to the punishment: Erfu's servant Wei was complicit in the killing, and both were permanently shackled to a mountain in the northwest — right feet fettered, hands and hair bound behind them, tied to a tree. The Emperor's punishment was total and irrevocable.

The implication is clear: killing Yayu was a cosmic offense. This creature, whatever danger it represented, had a protected status that Erfu violated. The Shanhaijing records no reason for the murder and no justification for the protection. It presents the fact, and the consequence.

## The Resurrection Problem

The Huainanzi, writing centuries later, places Yayu in a completely different context — as one of ten catastrophes that plagued the world during Emperor Yao's reign:

> Yayu, Zaochi, Jiuying, Dafeng, Fengxi, and Xiushe all became scourges upon the people. Yao sent Yi to kill Yayu.

The Shanhaijing's Yayu was already dead — murdered by Erfu. How is there a Yayu for Hou Yi to kill in Yao's time? The texts do not explain. Guo Pu, the fourth-century commentator, identified the Shanhaijing's 窫窳 and the Huainanzi's 猰貐 as the same creature, which creates a narrative: murdered by a god, it was somehow resurrected — by the Yellow Emperor's physicians (who could revive the dead), by its own divine nature, or by the simple logic that a wrongly killed creature does not stay dead — and the resurrection made it worse than it was before.

## Among the Ten Catastrophes

The Huainanzi's list of Hou Yi's enemies is one of mythology's great action sequences: in a single campaign, Yi shot nine suns from the sky, killed Zaochi (Chisel-Teeth), drowned Jiuying (Nine-Heads), snared Dafeng (Great Wind), and killed Yayu. The order places Yayu last among the terrestrial kills, after the solar catastrophe is resolved. This is narrative logic: the monster that started with a divine death, wrongly taken, ends with a heroic death, properly administered. Hou Yi is the corrective force.

See [Hou Yi](/creatures/hou-yi/) for the archer who ended Yayu's catastrophic career; see [Gonggong](/creatures/gonggong/) for another figure whose violence was too large for heaven to contain.
