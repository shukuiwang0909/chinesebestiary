## One Line, One God

The *Classic of Regions Beyond the Seas: South* spends seven characters on Zhurong (南方祝融，兽身人面，乘两龙): he presides over the south, he has a beast's body and a human face, and he rides two dragons. The line belongs to a matched set — east Goumang on two dragons, west Rushou with a snake in his left ear on two dragons, north Yuqiang with serpents at ears and feet. The four directional gods ride the same chassis in different trim, catalogued like officers of a single celestial bureaucracy. Zhurong's beast body (兽身, not Goumang's bird body) is the only clue the line gives — and it was enough for tradition to make him the fiercest of the four.

## "The Blaze That Goes On Shining"

The office came from the *Zuo Zhuan*: among the five rectifiers (正) of the five phases, the Fire Rectifier is called Zhurong. The *Discourses of the States* (国语) preserves the naming speech. Li, a descendant of Gaoxin, served as Fire Rectifier; his brightness was pure, generous, and great — illuminating the virtue of Heaven and Earth, shining upon the four seas. "Therefore he was called Zhurong": 祝 for continuation, 融 for blazing light — the blaze that goes on shining. A later stratum splits the man from the title: Chong and Li, grandsons of Zhuanxu, were ordered to separate heaven from earth — Li pressed down upon the earth and became Zhurong. By Han times the two figures had fused into the fire god everyone meant.

## Two Genealogies, One Fire

The Classic itself tells his family story twice, and the two versions do not agree. The *Great Wilderness: West* makes him grandson of Zhuanxu — Zhuanxu begot Laotong, Laotong begot Zhurong, Zhurong begot Prince Longqin, whose three-stringed qin music drew the birds and beasts to dance on Mount Yao. The *Within the Seas* instead wires him into the Flame Emperor's line: Zhurong descended and dwelt by the Jiang river, and begot Gonggong — the water god, the very element fire cannot abide. Later ages chose the feud over the family: the five-phase scheme casts fire and water as eternal opponents, and modern retellings love a Zhurong-versus-Gonggong duel. The compilers of the Classic, characteristically, left both truths on the page.

## Fire of the South, Ancestor of Chu

The *Seasonal Rules* of the *Huainanzi* give him his full court: in the southern palace, wearing red, feeding on millet and mutton, the god of the summer months — when the ruler must honor the fire phase and forbid all executions that would chill the rising yang. His cult ran deepest in the south. The ruling house of Chu claimed Zhurong as its direct ancestor, and the state temple of the fire god at Danyang was among its holiest sites. Long after the rectifiers faded from state ceremony, Zhurong kept a hearthside post: later tradition installed him among the candidates for god of the kitchen stove — the most domestic of gods, descended from the brightest of offices.

## Modern Echoes

In May 2021, China's first Mars rover touched down in Utopia Planitia — and it carried the fire god's name. The Zhurong rover, part of the Tianwen-1 mission, was named after the ancient god of fire and the south. The choice was deliberate poetry: to Chinese astronomers of old, Mars was Yinghuo, the "Sparkling Deluder," a red wandering star associated with fire and war. Who better to walk its rust-red deserts than the deity who tamed flame itself? The name also carried a quiet ambition: a figure from a text over two thousand years old, driving across another world in the twenty-first.
