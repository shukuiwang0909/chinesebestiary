## The Omen of State Power

The *Shanhaijing*'s creatures come in three rough omen categories: auspicious (eat me and you'll be healthy), inauspicious (see me and disaster follows), and neutral (this is what I look like; here is what I taste like). The Huahuai is firmly inauspicious, but what makes it unusual is the specificity of its omen: not flood, not drought, not war, but corvée labor.

大繇 (*dà yáo*) — great conscript labor. In early Chinese states, corvée was the mechanism by which the state mobilized human bodies for public works: walls, canals, palaces, tombs, roads. It was not paid. It was obligatory. It could last years. It took farmers from their fields at planting and harvest time. It killed people on construction sites. It broke families apart. In the Qin period, hundreds of thousands died building the Great Wall; in earlier dynasties, canal-digging campaigns mobilized comparable numbers. Corvée was one of the most widely feared of all state impositions.

The Huahuai sees this, and names it.

## The Sound

"Its sound is like the chopping of wood" (其音如斫木). This is not incidental. The Shanhaijing rarely describes sounds as specifically as this — most creatures' sounds are compared to other animals (like a dog, like a crow, like a thunder). Wood-chopping is a human sound: an economic activity, a construction sound, the sound of labor beginning.

The Huahuai's call is an announcement. Before the corvée mobilization orders arrive, before the officials knock on the door, the Huahuai is in the mountains, sounding like the work that is coming. It is a warning that takes the form of what it warns about.

## Two Necks

The anatomy is unexplained, as most Shanhaijing oddities are. Two necks on a pig body is not the flamboyant monstrousness of a nine-headed creature — it is a smaller, quieter wrongness. The pig is familiar; the extra neck is not. The Huahuai does not look like a divine beast. It looks like a pig that is slightly wrong, making a construction sound, and portending that your labor belongs to the state.
