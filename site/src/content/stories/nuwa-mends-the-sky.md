## When the World Broke

The *Huainanzi* (c. 139 BCE) keeps the account of the day creation came apart:

> 往古之时，四极废，九州裂，天不兼覆，地不周载。火爁焱而不灭，水浩洋而不息。猛兽食颛民，鸷鸟攫老弱。于是女娲炼五色石以补苍天，断鳌足以立四极，杀黑龙以济冀州，积芦灰以止淫水。苍天补，四极正，淫水涸，冀州平，狡虫死，颛民生。
>
> "In the most ancient time, the four pillars collapsed and the nine provinces split; the sky could not cover all beneath it, the earth could not bear all upon it. Fire raged and would not die; the waters spread and would not recede. Fierce beasts devoured the innocent people, and birds of prey seized the old and the weak. Thereupon Nüwa smelted five-colored stones to patch the blue sky, cut off the legs of the great tortoise to raise up the four pillars, slew the black dragon to rescue the land, and piled up reed ashes to stop the flooding waters. The sky was patched; the four pillars stood true; the floods dried; the land was level; the vermin died; and the people lived."

Notice what the passage does not have: a culprit. Later retellings blamed the catastrophe on Gonggong, the water god, cracking his head against Mount Buzhou and snapping heaven's column — a fine story, but a *different* text's story. The Huainanzi's sky simply fails, the way old things fail, and Nüwa simply repairs it. The goddess's work is maintenance, not vengeance: smelting, propping, damming. Five-colored stones for the vault of heaven — which is why, the tradition says, sunset clouds are streaked with color: you are looking at the patch.

## The Mud

The second story is quieter and is preserved in the *Fengsu Tongyi* tradition:

> 俗说天地开辟，未有人民，女娲抟黄土作人。剧务，力不暇供，乃引绳于泥中，举以为人。
>
> "It is commonly said that when heaven and earth first separated, there were no human beings. Nüwa pinched yellow clay into figures to make people. The work was great and her strength could not keep up — so she trailed a rope through the mud and flung the drops, and these too became people."

Every Chinese reader hears the class system being born in that second sentence: the hand-pinched are the noble, the rope-flung drops are the rest of us. The text does not say it. It does not have to.

## Why She Is Both

The two stories never meet in the old texts — the sky-mender and the mud-worker are not linked by any ancient narrator. Tradition simply holds both, the way it holds that she is Fuxi's sister and also his wife: the archive keeps variants because the variants are all load-bearing. The mender is the goddess of the world's maintenance; the potter is the goddess of its beginning. Between the two of them, Nüwa covers the whole job — she made humanity, and then she fixed the sky over it.
