*What it is, what the names mean, and why nobody can decide whether it is real*

In January 2026, Chinese state television "revealed" a 120,000-ton aerospace carrier, and Western defense media scrambled to file it. Was it a weapons program? Propaganda? A movie prop? The honest answer is the one nobody reached for: the **Nantianmen Project** (南天门计划, "South Gate of Heaven Project") is a science-fiction franchise — built not by a film studio but by the cultural arm of China's state aerospace giant — whose fleet is named, ship by ship, from the mythological bestiary this site catalogs.

This page is the stable explainer the English internet lacks: what the project is, what is in its fleet, what its names mean, and where the line between fiction and aspiration actually sits.

## What the Name Means

Nantianmen — the **South Gate of Heaven** — is the main entrance of the celestial palace in Chinese cosmology, the gate through which the Monkey King keeps forcing his way in *Journey to the West*, and the threshold where mortals' business becomes the gods'. To name a fleet "the South Gate of Heaven Project" is to announce, in four syllables, that the heavens are a place with a door — and that someone intends to hold it. (English searchers should note: the bare word "Nantianmen" also names a mountain in the game *Genshin Impact*; the aerospace project is always the full "Nantianmen Project" or "Nantianmen Plan.")

## Whose Project It Is

The project was launched around 2017 by **AVIC Global Culture**, a cultural subsidiary of the Aviation Industry Corporation of China (AVIC) — the state conglomerate that builds China's actual military aircraft. It is, on paper, a science-fiction IP: concept art, scale models, an official artbook (published April 2025), animated shorts, immersive exhibitions, and theme-park installations in Shanghai's Lingang district and Meizhou. Its fictional premise is set in the 2030s: Earth faces compound crises — geological catastrophes and an extraterrestrial threat — and humanity answers with a unified aerospace defense fleet.

The fleet made its public debut as large-scale models at the Zhuhai Airshow beginning in 2018, returned with a crewed-fighter mockup in November 2024, and in 2026 received two rounds of prime-time state-media "disclosures" plus a three-month solo exhibition, *Celestial Memory*, at Beijing's China Millennium Monument (July 8 – October 11, 2026).

## The Fleet, and What Each Name Means

Every platform in the fleet carries a name out of Chinese mythology — this is the layer defense journalism consistently skips.

| Platform | Role (in the fiction) | Published concept specs | The name's origin |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Luanniao** 鸾鸟 | 100,000-ton-class aerospace carrier, the flagship | 242 m long, 684 m wingspan, ~122,900 t max takeoff weight, carries up to 88 fighters | The [Luan Bird](/creatures/luan-bird/) of the *Classic of Mountains and Seas* — a five-colored omen-bird whose appearance guarantees peace under heaven |
| **Xuannü** 玄女 | Unmanned aerospace superiority fighter | 24.6 m long, 14.1 m span, 52 t MTOW, 14-day endurance, pulse particle-cannon pod, hypersonic missiles, full-spectrum stealth | [Jiutian Xuannü](/creatures/jiutian-xuannu/), the Mysterious Lady of the Nine Heavens — goddess of military strategy, who armed the Yellow Emperor for his decisive war |
| **Baidi** 白帝 | Crewed space-air fighter, stealth, manned/unmanned switchable | Mockup shown at Zhuhai 2024 (the only Nantianmen craft with its own English Wikipedia entry, as "AVIC Baidi Type-B") | The White Emperor — sovereign of the west and autumn in five-phase cosmology; the metal phase, whose domain is blades |
| **Chengying** 承影 | Manned tactical mech | Displayed as a 1:1 model at the 2026 Beijing exhibition | *Chengying*, "Bearing-a-Shadow" — one of the legendary swords in the *Liezi*, a blade so fine its shadow could barely be seen |
| **Zihuo** 紫火 | Utility VTOL platform, the newest addition | Design speed 700–800 km/h, adapted to low gravity and thin atmosphere | "Purple Fire" — in Daoist vocabulary the purple hue of the highest celestial realm and the fire of transcendence |

Read the roster slowly and a pattern emerges: this is not branding, it is *staffing*. The carrier is an omen of peace; the fighter is the goddess who hands down victory; the mech is an invisible sword. The fleet is organized like the celestial bureaucracy itself — each machine assigned a mythological officeholder appropriate to its duties, the same logic that put the fire god [Zhurong](/creatures/zhurong/) on Mars and the sun-mother [Xihe](/creatures/xi-he/) in orbit (see [Why China Names Its Spacecraft After Mythology](/articles/why-china-names-spacecraft-after-mythology/)).

## Fiction, Propaganda, or Roadmap?

Here is the part Western coverage gets wrong by insisting on a single answer.

- **As fiction**, it is real and elaborate: artbooks, model kits, animation, live exhibitions, a coherent 2030s universe.
- **As propaganda**, it functions whether or not a bolt is ever turned: state media broadcasts "specs" with a straight face, and the 2026 disclosure cycle reliably produces headlines abroad about a "confirmed 120,000-ton orbital carrier."
- **As roadmap**, individual technologies it showcases — hypersonics, reusable spaceplanes, directed energy — are genuine Chinese research areas, which is exactly what lets the ambiguity run.

The honest classification is that the Nantianmen Project is all three at once, in proportions that remain deliberately unstated. When a defense outlet reports the Luanniao as a weapons program, it is reporting the poster for a film as if it were the invasion.

## Why It Works as Myth-Making

Strip the controversy away and the project is a remarkable cultural artifact: a state aerospace corporation writing mythology forward instead of backward. For two thousand years the tradition assigned gods to heaven; the Nantianmen Project assigns hardware to the gods' old offices and hangs the fleet on heaven's own gate. The [Luan Bird](/creatures/luan-bird/) spent two millennia certifying peace with its plumage; in the fiction it keeps the peace with an air wing of eighty-eight fighters named for the [goddess of strategy](/creatures/jiutian-xuannu/) herself.

Whatever the project is, its names are real — and they are older than the nation that launched them.
