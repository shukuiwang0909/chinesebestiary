## 🦊 JIǓWĚIHÚ 九尾狐

**Nine-Tailed Fox (Jiuwei Hu)** · Wade-Giles: *chiu-wei hu*
Also known as: Nine-tailed Vulpine, Qingqiu Fox 青丘狐
Category: Auspicious Beast 祥兽 · Element: None · Four Perils: No · Four Divine: No

---

### 📜 Source Text 原文出处

**《南山经 · 青丘之山》** *(Classic of the Mountains: South)*

> 又东三百里，曰青丘之山，其阳多玉，其阴多青雘。有兽焉，其状如狐而九尾，其音如婴儿，能食人；食者不蛊。

*Original translation:*
Three hundred li further east lies Mount Qingqiu. Its southern slopes hold much jade; its northern slopes, much blue pigment. There is a creature there shaped like a fox with nine tails. Its cry sounds like a human infant, and it can devour people — yet those who eat its flesh are shielded from poison-hexes.

**《海外东经》** *(Classic of Regions Beyond the Seas: East)*

> 青丘国在其北，其狐四足九尾。

The land of Qingqiu lies to the north. Its foxes have four paws and nine tails.

**《大荒东经》** *(Classic of the Great Wilderness: East)*
The nine-tailed fox is recorded as an auspicious omen appearing in times of peace and good rule.

> 注：三处原文互相印证，构成本词条的"出处三角"——这是海外同类词条几乎从不做的，是我们的差异化信任资产。

### 📝 Summary

The nine-tailed fox (九尾狐, jiǔ wěi hú) is the most famous creature in the *Classic of Mountains and Seas*, first described around the 4th century BCE on Mount Qingqiu. Though the original text warns that it "can devour people," later Chinese tradition reversed its image completely: by the Han dynasty it was an omen of peace and flourishing descendants, and by the Tang dynasty it was a supernatural beauty. It is the ancestor of Japan's *kitsune* and Korea's *kumiho* — one fox, three mythologies.

### 🎨 Appearance

A fox the size of a large dog, covered in flame-red or golden-white fur, with nine full tails fanning behind it. Its eyes are described in later sources as golden, and its cry mimics a human baby — its hunting lure. Ming-dynasty illustrated editions show it seated like a fox but with tails layered in threes.

### ✨ Abilities

- **Voice of the infant** — lures victims by crying like a human baby
- **Poison immunity** — eating its flesh grants protection against gu hexes
- **Omen-sight** — appears only in times of peace and righteous rule
- **Shapeshifting** — a later (Han–Tang era) attribution: foxes living 1,000 years gain nine tails and human form

### 🏮 Symbolism

- **Nine tails = many descendants**: "nine" is the yang extreme; the fox became a wedding gift symbol for fertility
- **Moral mirror**: early texts treat it as a man-eater; the *Guangyi Ji* (9th c.) and later tales cast it as seductress; modern readings reclaim it as a powerful feminine spirit
- **Auspicious omen**: the *Bamboo Annals* records a nine-tailed fox appearing before King Yu of Xia — a sign of his destined kingship

### 🌏 Modern Location

Mount Qingqiu is traditionally placed near **Yishui, Shandong Province** (some scholars argue for a more southern location). The "State of Qingqiu" appears in several Warring States texts as an eastern coastal polity.

## 🎲 D&D 5E Stat Block

**JIUWEIHU (Nine-Tailed Fox)** — *Large celestial (shapechanger), neutral good*

> **Armor Class** 14 (natural)
> **Hit Points** 105 (10d10 + 50)
> **Speed** 50 ft.
>
> STR 16 (+3) · DEX 17 (+3) · CON 20 (+5) · INT 14 (+2) · WIS 16 (+3) · CHA 19 (+4)
>
> **Saving Throws** Dex +6, Con +8, Wis +6, Cha +7
> **Skills** Perception +6, Deception +7, Insight +6, Arcana +5
> **Damage Resistances** radiant; bludgeoning/piercing/slashing from nonmagical attacks
> **Condition Immunities** charmed, poisoned
> **Senses** truesight 30 ft., passive Perception 16
> **Languages** Celestial, Sylvan; telepathy 60 ft. (understands Common)
> **Challenge** 5 (1,800 XP) · **Proficiency Bonus** +3

***Shapechanger.*** The jiuweihu can polymorph into a Small or Medium humanoid it has seen, or back into its true form. Its statistics, other than size, are the same. Equipment it wears or carries isn't transformed. It reverts to its true form if it dies.

***Keen Hearing and Smell.*** Advantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on hearing or smell.

***Innate Spellcasting.*** The fox's spellcasting ability is Charisma (spell save DC 15). It requires no components:
- At will: *dancing lights, disguise self, friends*
- 3/day each: *charm person, invisibility, suggestion*
- 1/day each: *protection from poison, sanctuary*

**Actions**

***Multiattack.*** The fox makes two attacks: one with its bite and one with its tails.

***Bite.*** *Melee Weapon Attack*: +6 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. Hit: 8 (1d10 + 3) piercing damage.

***Nine-Tail Lash.*** *Melee Weapon Attack*: +6 to hit, reach 10 ft., one target. Hit: 10 (2d6 + 3) bludgeoning damage, and the target must succeed on a DC 15 Strength saving throw or be knocked prone.

***Foxfire (Recharge 5–6).*** Each creature in a 20-foot cone must make a DC 15 Dexterity saving throw, taking 21 (6d6) fire damage on a failure, or half as much on success. This fire sheds no heat and ignites nothing — it burns only what the fox wills.

**DM Lore Box**
> A jiuweihu does not lie — it simply asks questions until you deceive yourself. In the Celestial Bureaucracy they serve as auditors: sent to mortal courts to test whether a ruler's virtue is real. Killing one brings 1,000 years of ill fortune to the killer's bloodline; feeding one grants immunity to all poisons and hexes for a year. Either way, mortals who meet one rarely forget it.
